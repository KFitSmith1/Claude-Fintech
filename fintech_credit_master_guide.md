# PART A — One-Page Cheat Sheet: Build Both Credit Files on an ITIN

> **The core idea:** You have *two separate credit files* — a **business** file (D&B, Experian Business, Equifax Business) and a **personal** file (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Build them in parallel. As an ITIN-only founder, lean on products that (1) do **no personal credit pull**, (2) accept **ITIN or passport** at the identity step, and (3) **report ongoing positive history**.

### ✅ Step 0 — Foundation (week 1)
- Form a **US LLC/Corp** → get an **EIN** (ITIN qualifies you to request one).
- Get a **US business address** + **US business bank account**. ITIN/passport-friendly: **Mercury (Business)**, **Wise**, **Baselane**, **Comun**, **Majority**.
- Register a free **D-U-N-S number** (Dun & Bradstreet).

### 🏢 Build BUSINESS credit (no personal-credit exposure)
| Do this | Best picks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Open 3–4 **net-30 vendors** | **Crown Office Supplies** (all 3 bureaus), **Quill**, **Grainger** | Report positive history, EIN-only, no personal pull. ⚠️ *Avoid relying on Uline — may report negatives only* |
| Add a **reporting no-PG card** | **Ramp** (best no-SSN path), **Brex**, **Mercury IO** | All 3 business bureaus, no PG, **no personal credit pull at all** |
| Add a **credit-builder tool** | **Nav Prime** | Monthly tradeline to all 3 business bureaus |
| After 6–12 mo, **graduate to a lender** | **OnDeck**, **Amex Business Blueprint**, **Bluevine** | Larger limits, report ongoing positive to business bureaus |

### 👤 Build PERSONAL credit on an ITIN
| Do this | Best picks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Start with a **credit-builder** | **Self** (Builder + secured Visa) | All 3 consumer bureaus, **no credit check**, ITIN-friendly, two tradelines |
| Add a **secured/starter card** | **Majority** secured Visa, **Tilt** | Immigrant-built / ITIN-accepting (confirm bureaus) |
| Once you have a score | **Capital One Spark** (standard) | Rare business card that builds **personal** credit with ongoing positive reporting |

### 💸 Need capital without touching credit at all?
**Square Loans · Shopify Capital · Stripe Capital · PayPal Working Capital** — underwrite on your *platform sales*, no PG, no personal pull, and the first three **explicitly accept ITIN** (identity only). Trade-off: **they build no credit** and are invitation/offer-based.

### ⛔ Avoid / watch out
- **SBA loans** — 2026 rules require 100% US-citizen ownership (disqualifies ITIN-only).
- **Capital on Tap** — soft pull but **requires a PG**, so default hits personal credit.
- **Tomo** (not reporting in 2026), **Petal** (closed to new — now Tilt), **Sequin** (credit feature gone), **Stilt** (defunct).
- **"No-PG" ≠ "no SSN"** — almost every no-PG card still needs ITIN/passport for KYC identity. *Ask the issuer directly whether ITIN/passport satisfies the beneficial-owner check — that's the make-or-break step.*

### 🎯 The 5 best products for an ITIN-only founder
1. **Ramp** — no-PG card, documented no-SSN path, reports to all 3 business bureaus.
2. **Mercury (Business) + IO** — open with passport+EIN; IO reports to all 3 business bureaus, no PG.
3. **Self** — build personal credit on an ITIN (all 3 consumer bureaus, no credit check).
4. **Crown Office Supplies + Quill + Grainger** — EIN-only business tradelines.
5. **Stripe / Shopify / Square Capital** — ITIN-accepted financing on sales history, no credit pull.

# PART B — Companies & Products: No-PG, Soft-Pull Prequalification & ITIN Access

### How to read this report

Three terms get used loosely in the market. Getting them straight is the key to the whole report:

| Term | What it actually means | Why it matters for an ITIN holder |
|---|---|---|
| **No Personal Guarantee (no-PG)** | The business — not you personally — is liable for the debt. You don't pledge personal assets. | Common on cash-balance corporate cards. Does **not** by itself mean "no SSN needed." |
| **No personal credit pull** | The issuer never touches your personal credit file — not even a soft inquiry. Underwriting rests on business cash balance, raised capital, or platform sales. | **The best outcome** for a thin-file or ITIN-only applicant — your personal credit (or lack of it) is irrelevant. |
| **Soft-pull prequalification** | The issuer does a *soft* personal-credit inquiry (no score impact) to generate a real, non-binding offer. A *hard* pull lands only when you accept or draw funds. | Your personal credit **is** checked — just gently, and only until you commit. Still needs a personal identifier (SSN **or ITIN**). |

A critical, repeated finding: **"no-PG" and "no SSN" are different things.** Almost every legitimate no-PG corporate card still verifies the *identity* of a beneficial owner (KYC / USA PATRIOT Act). That identity step — not the underwriting — is where ITIN-only applicants hit friction.

> **The single most important takeaway:** For an ITIN-only founder, the strongest products are those that do **no personal credit pull at all** and accept an **ITIN or passport** at the identity step — because then neither your credit file nor your lack of an SSN is a gate. The next best are platform/embedded lenders that underwrite on your sales history.

### PART 1 — The Big Picture: Is Having Only an ITIN an Issue?

**Bottom line: Having only an ITIN is *not* a hard blocker, but it narrows your options and — as of mid-2026 — is becoming a bigger friction point.**

There are three distinct pathways into US business credit, and an ITIN-only founder can use all three:

1. **Personal-credit-based business cards** (e.g., Chase Ink, Capital One Spark, Amex Business). These explicitly accept an **ITIN in place of an SSN**, pull your personal credit, and require a personal guarantee. This is the *most firmly documented* ITIN path — but it requires a personal credit history.

2. **Cash-flow / no-PG corporate cards** (Brex, Ramp, Rho, Mercury IO, Stripe). These underwrite on your **business bank balance or raised capital**, not personal credit — so no PG and often **no personal credit pull at all**. The catch: they still collect a personal identifier for KYC. Whether they accept an ITIN/passport at that step varies by issuer and is the make-or-break variable.

3. **EIN-only business credit** (net-30 vendors + business bureaus). Builds a *business* credit file keyed to your EIN/DUNS — essentially no personal-identity barrier beyond the EIN, which an ITIN holder can obtain.

#### Why issuers ask for an SSN even on "business" products

The SSN request usually serves one of two *separable* functions:

- **Underwriting** — only relevant for products that pull your *personal* credit (path 1). Pulling credit requires a personal identifier, and bureaus *can* generate a report keyed to an ITIN.
- **Identity / KYC / AML** — required on virtually everything under the **USA PATRIOT Act (CIP rule)**, the **beneficial-ownership (CDD) rule**, and **OFAC** screening. For a *non-US person*, the CIP rule explicitly allows an **SSN, ITIN, EIN, or passport number** as a valid identifier. So banks *can* legally accept an ITIN — whether a given fintech *does* is a policy choice.

**Key insight:** On no-PG cash-balance cards, the SSN ask is almost always a KYC/identity requirement, not an underwriting one. That's the boundary where an ITIN-only founder's experience differs by issuer.

#### ⚠️ New 2026 regulatory headwind

A **May 2026 Executive Order, "Restoring Integrity to America's Financial System,"** directs federal regulators to treat customer immigration status as a financial-risk factor. It specifically states that **use of an ITIN to obtain credit products or open deposit accounts where the applicant lacks verified lawful immigration status may be flagged as a risk factor requiring enhanced due diligence.**

Critically:
- It does **NOT** ban ITIN accounts or ITIN credit applications, and does **not** require banks to close existing accounts or collect citizenship data from current customers (a mandatory-data-collection draft provision was dropped after industry pushback).
- Treasury was directed to issue a **red-flags advisory (~mid-July 2026)** and propose **BSA rule changes (~mid-August 2026)**.
- **Practical effect:** an ITIN-only applicant may face **more identity questions and enhanced due diligence**, especially without verified lawful status — but ITIN credit access remains legal.

*This is a fast-moving area. Reconfirm the current Treasury guidance before applying.*

### PART 5 — The EIN-Only Pathway: Build Business Credit Without an SSN

This is the most reliable, lowest-friction route for an ITIN-only founder because it leans on the *business* identity, not yours.

1. **Form a US LLC or corporation** and obtain an **EIN** from the IRS — an ITIN holder qualifies to request one.
2. **Get a free D-U-N-S number** from Dun & Bradstreet (tied to the EIN, not your SSN).
3. **Open net-30 vendor accounts** that report to business bureaus with EIN only (no personal credit check):
   - **Uline** — reports to all 3 business bureaus; needs business name + EIN; orders ~$50+ to be reported.
   - **Quill** — easy approval; EIN + ~30 days in business; reports to D&B.
   - **Grainger** — EIN + ~3 months in business; DUNS ideal.
   - **Crown Office Supplies** — no personal credit check; 90 days in business; active EIN.
4. **Add Nav Prime** to report 1–2 monthly tradelines to D&B, Experian Business, and Equifax Business.
5. The three **business bureaus** (D&B PAYDEX, Experian Business, Equifax Business) typically surface payment history after ~60–120 days.

This builds a **business** credit profile independent of your personal SSN/ITIN.

> **Note:** Avoid older guides citing **Nearside** (shut down 2022/23) or the **Tillful card** (acquired by Nav in 2023; card discontinued — use **Nav Prime** instead).

**Foundation (do first):**
1. Register a US LLC/corporation; get an **EIN** using your ITIN.
2. Secure a **US business address** and open a **US business bank account**. If traditional banks balk, use **Mercury (Business)**, **Wise**, **Comun**, or **Majority** — all open with ITIN/passport.

7. If you sell on a platform, pursue **Shopify Capital / Stripe Capital / Square Loans** — no credit pull, no PG, explicit ITIN acceptance (identity only).

9. For a term loan, use **Camino Financial's secured/micro business loan** (accepts ITIN-only, no min FICO).
10. **Avoid the SBA path** — 2026 rules require 100% US-citizen ownership.

**(c) If you need to build personal credit first:**
11. **Self** (credit-builder + secured Visa, ITIN OK, no hard pull) and consumer cards like **Tomo** / **Petal** (ITIN accepted) can establish a personal file that later unlocks personal-credit business cards (Chase Ink, Capital One Spark, Amex Business — all accept ITIN).

# PART C — Credit Bureau Reporting Matrix

### How to read this — five things that matter

1. **Business bureaus ≠ consumer bureaus.** Building *business* credit (D&B PAYDEX, Experian Business, Equifax Business) is a completely separate file from your *personal* credit (Equifax/Experian/TransUnion). Most fintech business products that report do so to **business** bureaus only.

2. **No-PG = usually nothing to your personal credit, ever.** Without a personal guarantee there's no individual liable, so true no-PG corporate cards generally report **nothing — positive or negative — to consumer bureaus, even on default**. The generic "a missed business-card payment hits your personal credit after 60 days" warning applies to **PG-backed** cards, not genuine no-PG cards.

3. **SBFE is not a bureau** — it's the Small Business Financial Exchange, a member data co-op that *feeds* D&B, Experian, Equifax, and LexisNexis. "Reports to SBFE only" means the data *can* surface in business reports but through a single, narrower channel.

4. **Reporting is voluntary.** No law requires any issuer or vendor to report. Practices change without notice — verify before relying on any single tradeline.

5. **"Default-only" is a trap for credit-builders.** A product that reports *only* missed payments (e.g., Uline, per several sources) can hurt you but won't build positive history. For building credit you want **ongoing positive** reporting.

> **The headline:** If your goal is to **build business credit with zero personal-credit exposure**, the cleanest, best-documented tools are **Brex, Ramp, Mercury IO, Relay, and Nav Prime** (all report ongoing positive history to all three major business bureaus, no PG). For a thin **personal** file on an ITIN, **Self** is the strongest confirmed builder.

### PART 6 — Strategy: Building Both Files on an ITIN

**To build BUSINESS credit (no personal-credit exposure):**
1. Foundation: US entity + EIN + **DUNS number**.
2. Open **3–4 net-30 vendors** that report positive — lead with **Crown Office Supplies** (all 3 bureaus), add **Quill** and **Grainger**. Avoid relying on **Uline** for positive history.
3. Add a reporting **charge card / tool**: **Ramp** or **Brex** (all 3 business bureaus, no PG, no personal pull) and/or **Nav Prime** (monthly tradeline to all 3).
4. After 6–12 months, graduate to a reporting **lender** (OnDeck, Amex Business Blueprint, Bluevine) for larger limits.

**To build PERSONAL credit on an ITIN:**
1. Start with **Self** (Credit Builder + secured Visa → all 3 consumer bureaus, no credit check).
2. Add **Majority** secured Visa or **Tilt** (if ITIN accepted).
3. Once a personal score exists, a **Capital One Spark** card can add ongoing positive *personal* reporting while also funding the business.

**Avoid for credit-building** (they report nothing): **Square Loans, Shopify Capital, PayPal Working Capital** — great for cheap, credit-invisible capital, but they build no file.

# Important Caveats & Methodology

- **Point-in-time, June 2026.** Fintech approval criteria, ITIN policies, product availability, and bureau-reporting practices change frequently and reporting is voluntary. **Confirm every detail directly with the provider before applying or relying on a tradeline.**
- **How this was researched.** Compiled from nine parallel research agents sweeping no-PG corporate cards, fintech business lenders and embedded platforms, deposit-focused neobanks, net-30 vendors, ITIN-access analysis, the soft-pull prequalification landscape, and credit-bureau reporting. Sources include issuer help centers and product pages, business-credit specialists (Nav, Credit Suite, Business Credit Workshop, Dun & Bradstreet), independent reviews (NerdWallet, Bankrate, Merchant Maverick, Finder, Kudos, United Capital Source), and regulatory/legal sources (FinCEN CIP guidance, Federal Register CDD rule, White House Executive Order, Mayer Brown, Time).
- **WebFetch was frequently blocked (HTTP 403)** on primary help-center pages (Mercury, Stripe, Ramp/Brex support, Nav, OnDeck, vendor sites). Many findings therefore rely on search-result snippets cross-referenced across reputable third parties. Items that could not be confirmed to a primary source are marked **unverified** throughout.
- **ITIN acceptance is firmly verified only where stated** — explicitly documented for Stripe, Shopify Capital, Square Loans, Bluevine (identity), Self, Tilt, Tomo (historically), Camino (secured), Comun, Majority, and Wise. For Ramp the verified path is "foreign passport instead of SSN." For Brex, Mercury IO, Rho, Meow, and most loan products, ITIN acceptance is **unverified** — treat as "ask first."
- **Highest-confidence reporting findings:** Brex, Ramp, Mercury IO, Relay, Nav Prime, Self, Crown, Quill, Grainger, OnDeck, Amex Blueprint, and the "report nothing" group (Square / Shopify / PayPal Working Capital).
- **Lowest-confidence / verify directly:** Rho's exact bureau list; Stripe Corp Card positive-vs-default; Emburse & Torpago full bureau lists; Backd, National Funding, and Mercury Working Capital (loan) reporting; Uline's positive-vs-default scope; Summa's availability; Wise Business Plans' D&B; Majority's specific consumer bureaus; Giggle's bureau type; Comun's credit product.
- **2026 status changes to note:** Brex acquired by Capital One (~April 2026); Torpago paused direct applications; Tomo not currently reporting; Petal closed to new applicants (now Tilt); Sequin discontinued credit-building; Tillful folded into Nav; Stilt defunct. The May 2026 Executive Order on ITIN/immigration banking is still being implemented (Treasury guidance due summer 2026).
- **Not legal or financial advice.** Consult a qualified advisor for your specific situation.
